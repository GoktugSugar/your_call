# Vision

## Logline
You die. In a formless afterlife, a chaotic trickster runs you through his game of questions with no right answer — and quietly builds a portrait of who you really are.

## Concept
A single-player narrative game of dark comedy. The player is a **judge** — a man who spent his life sizing up everyone else, now dead (drunk behind the wheel) and made the subject of the imp's game: a long, ordered stream of questions with **no right answer**, of every kind, from silly would-you-rathers to genuine gut-punch dilemmas, posed by a capricious narrator who never lets him forget the irony. The imp is not judging his morality; he is figuring out **who he is** — on the whole person, not just his ethics. There are no right answers and no way to die again; every question ends on a **"how others chose"** crowd reveal, and every choice quietly shapes a *profile*, a portrait of the kind of person the player turns out to be, that decides which of several endings he reaches. Rendered as animated black-and-white stick-figure doodles in a cosmic void, where every question gets its own animated ink consequence.

## Pillars
- No right answers — every question divides people or reveals the answerer, and is never scored good/bad.
- A portrait, not a verdict — the profile tracks *who you are* (a handful of characterful axes), never how good you are.
- How others chose is the hook — every question ends on the crowd split.
- Dark comedy that stings but doesn't wallow — the imp plays the profound and the idiotic with the same ceremony.
- Radical visual simplicity — void, figures, deliberate colour on an ink base, minimal UI.
- Achievable by a small team — reposable rigs, a tight verb set, a deferred backend.

## Scope
- Platform: PC / Steam (first target).
- Length: ~5–6 hours, single-player, linear run.
- A long, ordered run of questions, **mostly light and medium** (quick beats) with **heavy dilemmas as rare, spaced spikes** (full scene-length beats). The fun questions are the volume; the heavy dilemmas are the memorable spikes.
- Every question gets an animated ink consequence, **scaled by weight**: a quick fun beat for a light question, a full ~10-minute scene (dialogue, buildup, animated consequence, aftermath) for a heavy dilemma. The heavy scenes are the substantial authoring + animation units — budget accordingly.
- The exact question count and the light/medium/heavy ratio are still open (see `open-questions.md`); they drive the animation budget.

## Tone
Funny with real bite. The imp is the engine of the comedy, and his single voice — playing the profound and the idiotic with the same ceremony — is what ties the whole range of questions together; the heavy dilemmas supply the sting. Pace in waves — a run of lighter or absurd questions, then a gut-punch that lands harder for the breathers before it. Subtle fourth-wall winks, but the fiction never fully breaks.

## Positioning
The "trolley dilemma + how-others-voted" space already exists (Trolley Problem Inc., Dr. Trolley's Problem, and others), but those are almost all text-based question generators with no arc. This game's edge: an *animated narrative* — every question, silly or heavy, gets an animated ink consequence — with a profile-tracking imp, real profile-based endings, and question types beyond binary A/B. The "how others chose" reveal those games are built on is here too, front and centre, but as one beat inside an arc that reads who you are and pays it off in an ending.
