# Vision

## Logline
You die. In a formless afterlife, a chaotic trickster puts you through an escalating series of moral dilemmas — and quietly builds a portrait of who you really are.

## Concept
A single-player narrative game of dark comedy and moral philosophy. The player is a **judge** — a man who decided what others deserved for a living, now dead (drunk behind the wheel) and made to face ~25–35 impossible dilemmas himself, posed by a capricious narrator who never lets him forget the irony. There are no right answers and no way to die again; every choice shapes a *moral profile* that pays off in the ending. Rendered as animated black-and-white stick-figure doodles in a cosmic void.

## Pillars
- Genuine dilemmas — no right answers, never scored good/bad.
- A portrait, not a verdict — the profile tracks *what kind* of reasoner you are.
- Dark comedy that stings but doesn't wallow.
- Radical visual simplicity — void, figures, one accent colour, minimal UI.
- Achievable by a small team — reposable rigs, a tight verb set, a deferred backend.

## Scope
- Platform: PC / Steam (first target).
- Length: ~5–6 hours, single-player, linear campaign.
- ~25–35 dilemmas. At this count and runtime, each is a full ~10-minute *scene* (dialogue, buildup, animated consequence, aftermath), not a quick quiz question. Each dilemma is therefore a substantial authoring + animation unit — budget accordingly.

## Tone
Funny with real bite. The narrator is the engine of the comedy; the dilemmas supply the sting. Pace in waves — a lighter or absurd beat after each gut-punch, so the next one lands. Subtle fourth-wall winks, but the fiction never fully breaks.

## Positioning
The "trolley dilemma + how-others-voted" space already exists (Trolley Problem Inc., Dr. Trolley's Problem, and others), but those are almost all text-based question generators with no arc. This game's edge: an *animated narrative* with a profile-tracking narrator, a real ending, and interaction verbs beyond binary A/B.
