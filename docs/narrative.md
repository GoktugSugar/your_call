# Narrative & Moral Profile

## Setting
A cosmic void / abstract nowhere. Bonus: near-zero background art, so all focus lands on the figures and the single accent colour.

## Narrator
A recurring **chaotic trickster** — seemingly capricious, but secretly tracking every choice. The chaos is a mask; the ending reveals how closely they were paying attention. The narrator is the primary vehicle for the comedy and for reflecting the player's profile back at them.

## Player character
A **blank *moral* slate over a fixed past.** He was a **judge** who died **drunk driving** — crashed his car and killed himself. That backstory is fixed and is the narrator's ammunition; his moral *present* is blank, filled in by the player's choices (the game builds his profile from what the player does, not from a pre-written personality). Stick figures are androgynous, so the doodle still reads as a stand-in.

**Why a judge:** he spent his career deciding what other people deserved, and now he's the one being judged — the auditor audited. The narrator weaponises this throughout. The DUI sharpens it: an officer of the law who broke it fatally, and who very likely sentenced people for the exact crime that killed him. His own past rulings are fair game to throw back at him, and the ending can compare the man he was on the bench to the choices he made here.

**Tone guard:** aim the comedy at his *hypocrisy*, not at the tragedy of the death itself — that keeps the "stings but doesn't punch down" line.

"How he died" is a reusable thread the narrator mines all game. Reveal timing is still open (a cold open on the crash vs. a gradual reveal as a blank-slate amnesiac pieces it together) — see `open-questions.md`.

## Ending
Reflects the player's **moral profile** (below). Replay value comes from different profiles → different endings and different narrator treatment.

## The moral profile
Tracks **what kind of moral reasoner** the player is — *not* how good they are. Use axes where **neither pole is the good guy**:
- The one vs. the many
- Mercy vs. justice
- Ends vs. rules
- Order vs. freedom

Under the hood these map onto real leanings (utilitarian, deontological, etc.), so the ending can paint a portrait — "you always sacrificed the few for the many, and never once bent a rule" — **without ever scoring the player.**

**Explicitly rejected:** a selfish↔selfless axis. It smuggles a "right answer" back in (selfless = good) and kills the point of a dilemma.

The profile is tracked silently — it is **not** shown as a running gauge during play (see `art-and-ui.md`). The exact final list of axes is still open (see `open-questions.md`).
