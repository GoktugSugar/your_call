# Narrative & Moral Profile

## Setting
A cosmic void / abstract nowhere, seen from the player's **own point of view**: the void is his perspective, not a stage he is shown standing on. Bonus: near-zero background art, so all focus lands on the figures and their sparing marks of colour.

## Narrator (the imp)
A recurring **chaotic trickster**, now **embodied on screen as a small hand-drawn imp**: a little devil in hand-drawn ink (horns, a tail, a grin). It scribbles into being in the void and from then on delivers all of the narration; the reused inked-in narrator text is its voice, and it stirs while the words appear so each line reads as coming from it. It is a **gleeful tormentor** who enjoys the player's confusion, teases, and savours the game it is running. It seems capricious but is secretly tracking every choice; the chaos is a mask, and the ending reveals how closely it was paying attention. It is the primary vehicle for the comedy and for reflecting the player's profile back at them.

The imp **explains only a little and stays cryptic.** It lets the player know he is dead and that it has a few questions, a little game, for him, but stays vague about why he is here, what is at stake, what happens after, and what the imp itself is (cagey even about a name; see `open-questions.md`). Enough that he gets the gist, no more.

**Register and language.** The imp's voice is dark comedy and the game is **not PG**: it swears where the tone fits (strong language used deliberately, not gratuitously). The clearest case is the secret **shoot-the-imp** ending (see `gameplay.md`): shot by the player, the imp rounds on the camera and says, exactly, *Oh, you absolute fucking rat. You shot me. You shot the fucking referee.* Then it fades and the credits roll.

## Player character
A **blank *moral* slate over a fixed past.** He was a **judge** who died **drunk driving** — crashed his car and killed himself. That backstory is fixed; his moral *present* is blank, filled in by the player's choices (the game builds his profile from what the player does, not from a pre-written personality). **He is never rendered as a figure:** the game is his point of view, so the void is his perspective and the imp addresses the player directly (the camera). The stick figures on screen are the *other* souls in each dilemma, androgynous stand-ins, never him.

**Why a judge:** he spent his career deciding what other people deserved, and now he's the one being judged — the auditor audited. The imp returns to this throughout. Crucially, in the void he holds no office: he was a judge in life, but here he is the one being put through it, made to choose for other souls by the imp, never an authority presiding over the afterlife. His own past rulings are fair game to throw back at him, and the ending can compare the man he was on the bench to the choices he made here.

**The DUI is a light recurring irony, not a thread to pull.** A judge who died drunk now being judged is a joke the imp lands once, quickly (a sniff, a smirk, dropped immediately), and returns to only as an occasional wink across the game. It is deliberately **not a mystery, not a twist, and not a guilt arc**, and it is **not** the narrator's heavy "ammunition." He killed only himself, so there is no victim to uncover and nothing to investigate. Aim the comedy at his *hypocrisy*, never at the tragedy of the death itself — that keeps the "stings but doesn't punch down" line.

**How he died is shown early, not withheld.** The game opens on a quick, fragmentary ink flash of the crash and he wakes foggy and half remembering (see *The opening* below). It is established lightly up front rather than saved as a reveal, which is what lets it stay a passing gag instead of a suspense thread.

## The opening
A short authored sequence introduces the imp and chains the first dilemmas with narration between them (the reusable beat structure is in `gameplay.md` and `tech.md`). In order:
1. **The crash.** A brief, impressionistic ink flash of the death — lights bearing down, a swerve, the hit — over black, fragmentary because he barely has it himself, then a cut to black.
2. **Waking.** He comes to. There is no body to show, so it plays as point of view: the black clears unevenly and the view drifts and steadies, as if his eyes are focusing, then the imp resolves into being. Brief, carried by motion rather than a figure.
3. **The imp.** It scribbles into being and takes over the narration: he is dead, it has a game for him, and it stays cryptic about the rest. It lands the DUI gag once and drops it.
4. **First dilemma:** the clean classic trolley (five over one), which teaches the loop.
5. **The taunt:** the imp dismisses that as too easy and pivots to a harder one.
6. **Second dilemma:** the whose-life-counts trolley (see `gameplay.md`), where the clean headcount no longer settles it.
7. **Transition** into the blue/red button dilemma as the next beat.
8. **Transition and the last bear.** A short imp hand-off, then the **man and the last bear** (see `gameplay.md`): the guide puts a gun in the dead judge's hands and makes him fire on one of two, needling him as ever about the life he spent deciding what others deserved, only now the other life on the scale is a whole species' future rather than one more person. Reached after the blue/red button, it widens the questions to non-human life and to the generations not yet born.

## Ending
Reflects the player's **moral profile** (below). Replay value comes from different profiles → different endings and different narrator treatment.

## The moral profile
Tracks **what kind of moral reasoner** the player is — *not* how good they are. Use axes where **neither pole is the good guy**:
- The one vs. the many
- Mercy vs. justice
- Ends vs. rules
- Order vs. freedom

Under the hood these map onto real leanings (utilitarian, deontological, etc.), so the ending can paint a portrait — "you always sacrificed the few for the many, and never once bent a rule" — **without ever scoring the player.**

**Explicitly rejected:** a selfish↔selfless axis. It smuggles a "right answer" back in (selfless = good) and kills the point of a dilemma.

The profile is tracked silently — it is **not** shown as a running gauge during play (see `art-and-ui.md`). The exact final list of axes is still open (see `open-questions.md`).
