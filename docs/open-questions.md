# Open Questions

Unresolved decisions. Move an item into the docs + `decisions.md` once settled.

- **Final title.** Working title is *Your Call*; final not locked (and should be checked for availability before release).
- **Palette / colour identity.** Settled (see `decisions.md`, 2026-07-07): the world is black ink on a white paper ground, and colour is used deliberately as an accent on that base for emphasis and meaning (amber, the blue/red buttons, the red-outlined wretches, the brown bear), never blanket decoration, with no hard limit on which colours. The old "exactly one amber accent" rule and the per-element exception bookkeeping are retired. What stays open is only taste: which specific colour choices to keep (the amber imp and the brown bear, below).
- **Blue/red button dilemma (first pass).** The literal blue/red button, framed as the guide making the dead former judge choose for one soul while the room has already pressed: press red and that soul lives for certain, press blue and it lives only if the room's blue fraction reaches the 0.50 threshold (seeded at 0.43). Two round, unlabelled buttons identified by colour alone (the one documented colour exception), named aloud by the guide. Copy, seed, and staging are a first pass and tunable. Resolved: the death-frame and the afterlife-authority tone (he is tested, not presiding), and the stats amber marks the pick.
- **Final moral-profile axes.** Which of (one vs. many, mercy vs. justice, ends vs. rules, order vs. freedom) ship, and how many. The per-choice axis mappings so far (trolley: one/many and ends/rules; whose-life-counts trolley: one/many and mercy/justice; gamble: order/freedom and one/many; the man and the last bear: one/many and ends/rules) are first pass and tunable.
  - **Candidate new axes to watch: human vs. non-human and present vs. future.** Both are raised by the man-and-the-last-bear dilemma (one present human life against the whole unborn future of a non-human species) but are mapped for now onto the existing one/many and ends/rules axes rather than given their own. Add either only if enough later dilemmas exercise it to make it read as a real dimension of the reasoner rather than a one-off; otherwise keep folding these tensions into the existing four. Neither pole would be the good guy (a requirement for any axis, see `narrative.md`).
- **Death reveal timing.** Settled: shown up front as a quick, fragmentary crash flash that cuts to black, with the player waking foggy and half remembering as the black clears into the void (point of view, no figure; see `narrative.md` and `decisions.md`, 2026-07-07). Kept a light passing gag, not a reveal. Left here only until locked.
- **The imp's name.** The guide is now embodied as the imp (small hand-drawn devil; see `narrative.md`). It stays cagey about a name in the opening as a deliberate bit; whether it ever gets a name later, or keeps refusing one, is open.
- **The imp's look, size, and liveliness.** First pass: a small black-ink devil at roughly 1.5x with a slow bob, a rocking lean, a subtle breath, and a nod-and-lean gesture while speaking, all exposed as `[SerializeField]` tunables on `ImpFigure`. It **repositions once per dialogue box** (`ImpRoamer`), hopping springily to a fresh spot and then holding to deliver, always clear of content via bounds-based keep-outs. Hop feel, roam bounds, and minimum clearance are tunable and likely to be refined in playtesting.
- **Amber imp (colour choice, in progress).** The imp is tinted the amber accent via its single exposed `ImpFigure._color` field, now on **both** the opening's imp and the menu's (they match). Under the loosened palette rule this is ordinary deliberate colour, not a rule break; what is open is only taste: keep the amber imp (it pulls the eye to the narrator) or revert to black ink (one field change, per instance). First read: legible and eye-catching, but softer and lower-contrast than the crisp black-ink figures. Decide in playtesting.
- **Brown bear (colour choice, in progress).** The last bear is filled with a muted brown wash inside her outline (`Palette.InkBrown` on `BearFigure`). Ordinary deliberate colour under the loosened rule; open only as taste, keep the brown or adjust its tone / opacity (a field change, or the fill off). The broader "palette identity" question that used to sit here is now settled, see the palette item above and `decisions.md` (2026-07-07).
- **Endings and credits (first pass).** The reusable ending scaffolding (`Ending` + `CreditsRoll`) and the secret shoot-the-imp joke ending are a first pass, meant to be reused by the real profile-based endings later. The credits content and roles (all placeholder), the scroll speed, the return-to-start behaviour, and the ending timings are tunable; the real endings and how the profile selects them are still to design.
- **Placeholder audio.** The background music (`Assets/Audio/Music/bgm_placeholder.mp3`) and the six imp voice blips (`Assets/Audio/SFX/ImpVoice/`) are placeholders to replace with a final track and a final imp voice. The blip clips were **reworked lower and warmer** and the playback retuned to suit (a narrow pitch spread so it reads as a talky mutter, and a blip guaranteed on the first revealed character); level, pitch range, and characters-per-blip stay exposed (first pass). Still placeholder. The music and SFX levels are scaled by the player's saved settings (see below).
- **Imp expressions and the menu speak-then-hop (first pass).** The imp now has a **toggleable facial-expression set** (`ImpFigure._useExpressions`, off by default; five on-model faces: grin, wide grin, smug, glare, surprised), and the menu imp uses it (a bark carries a face). Open: whether to roll expressions out to the **opening and dilemma imps** too (they keep the single face for now), and whether the set wants expanding or its looks refining. Also first pass and exposed: the menu bark shows as **plain floating ink text over the imp** (no bubble; the `SpeechBubble` was retired and is unused), positioned above or below the imp on `FloatingBarkText`, and the **speak-then-hop** cadence (the unhurried pause and the hold on `MenuImpBarker`; between barks the imp hops with the campaign roaming motion, `ImpRoamer.HopToNewSpot()`, not a teleport). The menu imp plays its pop-in entrance on load, like elsewhere. Tune in playtesting.
- **Save / resume system (for Continue).** The main menu's **Continue** is present but disabled and greyed, gated on a save existing (`MainMenuController.SavePresent()`, which returns false for now). Building a save/resume system was out of scope for the menu pass; when it lands, Continue enables itself with no other change. Open: what a save captures (the position in the sequence/campaign and the silent moral profile — settings already persist separately in `PlayerPrefs`), the format and slot count, and when it writes (per dilemma, on quit, checkpointed). No fail state, so a save is only a resume point, never a score.
- **Which settings to add.** The settings panel currently scopes to **music volume, SFX volume, and fullscreen**, persisted via `PlayerPrefs` and loaded on boot (a first pass, in the ink style). `GameSettings` and the panel are built to extend; candidates to add later: **text speed** (called out in the brief), resolution / quality, a master volume, language, and key rebinding. The panel's contents are now laid out **cleanly centred** (a stacked centred label above each centred control); its exact sizing and the SFX-preview cue stay open as taste. The menu imp is now **amber** (matching the opening) and speaks in an **ink speech bubble** then **teleports** (see the imp-expressions item above); its bark pool (about ten short lines) and the pacing are exposed and a first pass to tune in playtesting.
- **Dialogue box vs. minimal UI.** The narration sits in a stylised hand-drawn ink frame that **floats clear of the bottom edge** (`DialogueBox`), rounder and cleaner with a controlled wobble, holding **centred** text in the **Caveat** handwriting font; long narration **pages by whole sentences** inside it. The one softening of the "no boxes" rule (see `art-and-ui.md`). The bottom offset, box size and corner rounding, the Caveat weight (currently SemiBold) and font size, and the typing speed are all a first pass, tunable — the font size and box sizing especially, since together they set how many sentences land in one box (and thus the page count).
- **Advance pacing.** Narration is now advanced by click or key, one dialogue box at a time (and the imp repositions per box). The aftermath also has a **click gate** between the choice/consequence (with the imp's reaction) and the stats reveal, so the choice lands before the room's verdict; the reaction hold that gates it, the typing speed, and page lengths are all a first pass to tune in playtesting. Whether some narration should auto-advance after a hold instead of always waiting for input is still open.
- **Trolley layout and the branch (first pass).** The trolley tableau is composed centred, and the branch is a schematic right-angle switch (main straight through the fork; the branch drops at a right angle and runs parallel below), with the trolley staying level through the corner. The branch depth, the lone figure's spot on it, the group spacing, and whether the trolley stays level or banks are exposed on the `Track` / `Trolley` and tunable. The five wretches' small black doodle horns (whose-life-counts trolley only) are part of that dilemma's figure setup; their look is a first pass.
- **The man and the last bear (first pass).** A binary first-person shooting dilemma: one ordinary man against the last breeding female of her species, aimed at with an ink crosshair (see `gameplay.md`, `tech.md`). First-pass and tunable: the **animal (a bear)**; the seed split (42 shoot the man / 58 shoot the bear); the imp's copy; the two figures' placement, scale, and the bear's proportions; the crosshair reticle (its POV gun barrel is a tunable, now off by default); the two figures' size (bumped up) and their raised upper-middle placement; the aim/hit boxes and the amber lock feel; and the placeholder gunshot's volume (all exposed on `ShootingConsequence`, `Crosshair`, `AimHighlight`, `BearFigure`, and `GunSfx`). The bear was **redrawn clean and minimal** after the first pass read creepy, and the **cub cue was dropped** so the species weight rests on the imp's words alone (see `decisions.md`, 2026-07-07). Open for playtesting: whether the words alone land the future-of-the-species weight, and whether another animal reads better than a bear. The figures were later enlarged again (scale 1.45) to fill the space, the bear given a muted brown fill, and a secret **shoot-the-imp** joke ending added (see `decisions.md`, 2026-07-07).
