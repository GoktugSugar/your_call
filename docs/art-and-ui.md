# Art, UI & Audio

## Visual style
Black-and-white, hand-drawn **stick figures** — a doodle aesthetic. Characters are built on **reposable 2D skeletal rigs** (Unity's 2D Animation, bones and sprite skinning): one rig reposed into hundreds of scenarios rather than bespoke art per scene. This is the core reason the game's scope is achievable, so treat "repose the rig" as the default and "draw new art" as the exception.

The setting is a cosmic void — mostly empty space — so backgrounds are minimal and the figures carry every scene.

Non-human figures follow the same rule: clean, minimal ink doodles, not detailed drawings. The bear in the man-versus-bear dilemma is front facing and sitting, built from a rounded body, a head circle, two ears drawn as **open bumps on the head outline** (not full circles laid over it, which read as creepy), a small muzzle, and a **deliberately minimal face** (two dot eyes, a small nose, a tiny smile). The sparse face is what keeps her friendly rather than unsettling. She carries no cub or offspring cue on screen; the weight of her being the last of her kind rests entirely on the imp's words (see `gameplay.md`).

**Every question gets an animated ink consequence, scaled by its weight:** a quick fun beat for a light would-you-rather, a full drawn scene for a heavy dilemma. This animated payoff on *every* question, silly or grave, is the game's edge over text-based question games, so even a light question resolves into a small drawn moment rather than just text. Budget the heavy scenes as the expensive units (see `gameplay.md`).

**Would-you-rather breathers are illustrated, not full animated scenes.** The light would-you-rather palate cleansers (see `gameplay.md`) use a fast **two-doodle** presentation rather than a full scene: **two small hand-drawn ink doodles** sit side by side, one per option, each with a short **label** under it, clearly a this-or-that, in the same black-ink-on-paper doodle style (built from the shared ink strokes with the living-line boil, like every figure). A small **or** sits between them; the hovered side lifts and brightens a touch; the chosen side gets a brief **amber** ink flourish (the light-beat consequence). Nothing folds, no elaborate scene, so it stays snappy. Each doodle is a small on-model parametric drawing (for the starting pool: a poop swirl and an ice cream cone, a winged figure and a flexing figure, a giant duck and a little herd of tiny horses, a rewind button and a pause button, a snowflake and a sun, a three-armed figure and a two-headed figure), referenced from the question data as a `DoodleKind`, so a new one is data plus its two doodles (see `tech.md`). Each doodle draws its **ink outline in a colour matched to what it depicts** (a coloured line, never a filled or painted fill): the snowflake ice blue, the sun warm orange, the duck green, the poop and the horses brown, the ice cream pink, the winged figure sky blue, the flexing figure red, the rewind and pause buttons blue and orange, and the odd body doodles green and purple. Muted hand-drawn colours on the paper ground (`Palette.InkGreen` / `InkOrange` / `InkSky` / `InkPurple` alongside the existing brown / pink / blue / red), so each option reads with a hint of colour while staying in the doodle line style; the colour is the doodle's own (`InkDoodle.DefaultColorFor`) and a question can override it per option as data. Consistent with the loosened palette (deliberate colour on the ink base), amber still reserved for the hover and the flourish. Some doodles read best as a **head-only crop** rather than a tiny full body: the money chapter's animal pair (the insulting cat and the lying dog) are drawn as **just their heads**, the pointy-eared, whiskered cat set against the floppy-eared, big-nosed, muzzled dog, which stays clear and legible at the side-by-side option size where a whole small animal would not.

## Minimal, not sterile (stylisation)
Minimal governs how many elements are on screen (few, uncluttered). Stylisation governs the character of the elements you keep. The hand-drawn ink aesthetic is the style, so the game should read as spare but never plain or sterile. Character comes from four levers, none of which add clutter:
- A living ink line. Variable-weight, slightly rough, hand-drawn strokes, not clean geometric vector. A subtle boil (cycling two or three near-identical line frames) makes everything feel drawn and alive for almost no cost.
- Paper, not blank white. A faint paper or newsprint texture on the white ground so it reads as a sketchbook page rather than an empty screen. Still monochrome, still minimal.
- Characterful type. The narrator's text uses a distinctive hand-lettered typeface and inks in rather than hard-cutting, so the words carry voice. The previously deferred characterful narrator font is now adopted: **Caveat** (a handwriting font, `Assets/Fonts/Caveat/`, imported as a TMP SDF font asset and scoped to the narration for now), chosen at a weight and size that read clearly for full sentences (SemiBold), favouring readability over flourish. The whole line is laid out before it types and the glyphs are then revealed in place (by vertex alpha), so centred text never shifts a pixel while it inks on. Not a plain system font. It is a **dynamic** TMP atlas that grows on demand from the Caveat source, and the full **Turkish** character set (the dotted and dotless i, the breve g, the cedilla s, and the rest) is covered by the source and pre-baked into the atlas, so Turkish (and any language whose glyphs the source carries) renders with no missing glyphs, in the same handwritten hand (see `tech.md`, localization).
- Expressive, ink-like motion. Elements draw themselves on and wipe or dissolve like ink; transitions have easing personality; the amber reads like a marker stroke rather than a flat fill; figures keep a tiny idle life (a breath, or the boil).

What this does not mean: added UI chrome, decorative flourishes that compete for attention, extra colours, or busier screens. The minimal-UI rules below still hold. Stylisation lives in the line, the texture, the type, and the motion, never in more stuff on screen.

## Colour
- **Black ink on a white (paper) ground.** The world is monochrome hand-drawn ink on paper; that is the base identity and it must always read that way, a sketchbook page, not a full-colour scene.
- **Colour is used deliberately, as an accent on that base, for emphasis and meaning, never blanket decoration.** There is no hard limit on which colours or how many; the discipline is that colour stays purposeful, and the moment it turns decorative it stops meaning anything. The colours currently in play: **amber** for attention and consequence (a highlight, the lever, the aim lock, a stressed word); the **blue and red** of the blue/red button dilemma; **red** outlines marking the damned wretches in the whose-life-counts trolley; and a muted **brown** filling the last bear. These are ordinary deliberate-colour use, not exceptions to bookkeep.
- **The blue/red buttons.** The reframed viral dilemma uses hand-drawn blue and red on its two buttons and its "how the room voted" stats bars: a muted, inky crayon wash inside the black ink linework, never flat digital UI colour; hover and selection deepen the button's own colour. The colours live as `Palette.InkBlue` and `Palette.InkRed`. Deliberate colour for meaning (the buttons ARE their colours). See `decisions.md` (2026-07-06).
- **Evil figures are drawn red.** Any figure the imp brands a wretch, villain, murderer or guilty party is drawn with a **red** outline (its whole ink line is red, `Palette.InkRed`, the same red as the buttons and the guilt words, kept in the doodle line style), so it reads at a glance; ordinary and innocent figures stay black. Consistent across the trolleys: all five in The Marked Ones, only the murderer (not the innocent beside him) in The Passenger, only the currently cruel man (not the reformed killer, a deliberate irony) in The Reformed and the Cruel. The red is the imp's own branding, not proof of anything, only its word made visible. Exposed on `Figure` (`_brandColor`); which figures are red is per-dilemma data (`figuresBranded` for all of the main group, or `brandedMainIndices` / `brandedSpurIndices` for specific ones). (This began as the red-outlined wretches, replacing earlier red devil horns; see `decisions.md`, 2026-07-07 and 2026-07-08.)
- **Sleeping figures show drifting z-marks.** A sleeping figure carries a few small hand-drawn "z" marks that rise from the top-right of its head, drifting up and out and fading on a gentle loop, in the ink style, so it reads as asleep (The Sleeping's two on the main track; the awake one on the branch has none). Small and on-model; they stop the instant the figure is struck. Driven by per-dilemma data and a small `SleepZ` prefab (see `tech.md`).

Deliberate colour for meaning (pink = love), on the ink base. The parent (The Parent) likewise carries a short floating line about their children, reusing the plea treatment. Driven by per-dilemma data and a small `HeartFloat` prefab (see `tech.md`).

- **The confession booth, the confessors filing in and out, and the three verdicts (Chapter 7).** Chapter 7 (The Confession Booth; see `gameplay.md`) is a **fixed booth scene**, the one set piece drawn once and held for the whole docket, so the chapter reads as a fixed location with a conveyor of cases rather than a fresh scene per beat. The **`ConfessionBooth`** is an on-model hand-drawn ink confessional built from the shared ink strokes like the Track and the buttons: an outer frame, a doorway the confessor stands in, a **latticed grille screen** (the confessional cue), and a peaked roof. It **inks in once** on the first case (an idempotent reveal) and then just sits there, alive on the subtle boil, and registers a renderer-bounds keep-out so the roaming imp never sits on it. Each confessor is a plain on-model ink **`Figure`** who **files in** from one side (a light slide plus ink-in) to stand in the booth doorway, and **files out** to the far side after the verdict (a light slide plus ink-out), so the docket reads as a conveyor of souls. Their core position floats over them as a short **headline** in ink with no bubble (the same ink-over-a-figure treatment as the pleas, via the shared `FigurePleaPlacement`), while the imp announces the case and the confessor states their side in the bottom **dialogue box**. The **three verdicts** sit as a row of three ink text labels in the **choice-affordance area above the dialogue box** (clear of the booth and the figure), brighter on hover, **no amber** (amber stays for the chosen-verdict flourish and the reveal pick), picked by click or the 1/2/3 keys, the same consistent choice affordance the trolley and three-track use. The chosen verdict gets the usual amber **`InkPoof`** flourish; the how-others-chose reveal is **three reflective bars** on the shared `StatsView` (the player's pick in amber), the seeded three-way split. The **button gag** (case 4) draws the reused **`WalkingButton`** filing into the booth in place of a figure (it slides in, comes alive, confesses in its own floating ink voice, and walks off), and the **two stories** closer draws **two** confessors flanking the booth, one filing in from each side, as the two sides of the dispute. The booth and the confessors stay **black ink on the paper ground**; amber appears only on the verdict flourish and the reveal pick.
- **Coloured words in the narration, a consistent colour-to-meaning system.** The dialogue colours a few meaningful words per line by a **fixed mapping**, applied the same way every time so it reads as a system, not decoration, and stops the lines feeling like plain text:
  - **[red]** marks **death and the guilty**: killing and dying, and the wretched, the villain, the murderer, and the words that name them (kill, dies, dead, folds, murderer, killer, guilty, rotten, liars, cheats, monster, cruel, vicious). It is the same muted red as the red-outlined figures and the red button.
  - **[amber]** marks the **stakes and the emphasis**: the counts (five, one, two, three) and the weight of the choice, used sparingly for genuine emphasis (the accent).
  - **[pink]** marks the **player's partner and love** (the loved one in Someone You Loved), matching their pink figure and heart.
  - **[gold]** marks **the best life** (the golden crowned figure in Chapter 2's better-offer button), matching its warm gold.
  - **element colours** where a word *names* something already coloured in the game: in the blue/red button dilemma, and only there, the words for the red and blue buttons render red and blue, echoing the buttons; and the word for the golden best life renders gold (the `[gold]` above), echoing the crowned figure.
  No word ever gets any other, invented colour. Emphasis is authored inline in the copy as a simple tag the writer applies (`[amber]`, `[red]`, `[blue]`), chosen per line in the data, not hardcoded, routed through the same `Palette` by `InkMarkup`, with restraint (a few words per line, not everything). This stays inside the deliberate-colour discipline (colour for meaning, on the ink base). The whose-life-counts second trolley colours the imp's damning labels for the five (rotten, liars, cheats, stains, monsters) in **red** now, since they name the guilty (they were amber before the mapping was made consistent). The markup **travels per language**: a translation carries its own tags, so each language marks the equivalent words in its own line (see `tech.md`, localization). The tags are applied after the string is localized, so the colour mapping works identically in every language.

## UI / HUD philosophy — clear and simple
The interface recedes so the void and the figures dominate. Concretely:
- **No large HUD.** No persistent on-screen meters, bars, or status clusters. In particular, the profile is **not** shown as a running gauge during play.
- **No big menus.** Menus are minimal and sparse — only what's essential. The one appropriate place for a real menu is the **main menu and its settings screen** (`MainMenu.unity`; the game boots into it): these are the deliberate, in-style exception, kept hand-drawn and spare in the ink aesthetic (the Caveat hand, a rounded ink frame, amber only on the current selection and an on-toggle), never chrome UI. The imp lives on the menu too, in its **amber** tint (matching the opening). Instead of the bottom dialogue box or a speech bubble, the menu shows the imp's barks as **plain ink text floating directly over it** (Caveat, ink colour, typed with the blip voice), with **no bubble, box, tail, or background**, just the words on the paper, kept clear of the title and the options (above the imp, or below if there is no room). The imp **plays its pop-in entrance** when the menu loads (the same scale-and-bounce scribble-in it uses elsewhere), then keeps its idle life, and moves on a **speak-then-hop** cadence: it sits, and every so often (an unhurried pause, not often) says one short bark over its head in place; the text types out, holds, and clears, and only then does the imp **hop** to a fresh spot with the **same springy roaming motion the campaign imp uses** (an eased hop across the page, not an instant jump and not a teleport poof), staying clear of the title, the options, and the panel. A bark can carry a **facial expression** (a smug line shows a smug face), from the small toggleable set below. It is menu flavour, never blocking input, and the player can navigate at any time. The settings panel's contents are laid out **cleanly centred** (each label centred above its control), and include a **language** row (a left/right cycler in the same ink style, listing the languages by their own names) alongside the volumes and fullscreen. Every settings and menu label is drawn from the localization layer and re-localizes live when the language changes; the imp's barks and all narration switch to the chosen language as they next appear. See `gameplay.md` and `tech.md`.
- **The imp's face is a small toggleable expression set.** The imp can carry one of a few on-model faces, each just a different eyes-and-mouth line configuration in its own ink line (a default grin, a wide grin, a smug side-eye, a glare, a surprised round-eyed look). It is drawn light: a neutral default with the ability to set one (the menu imp does, per bark). The whole thing sits behind a single master toggle (`ImpFigure._useExpressions`); off, the imp keeps its original single face exactly. On the menu now; whether the opening imp adopts expressions too is open (see `open-questions.md`). The smug face's mouth was dropped a little so it has the same clear gap below the eyes as the others. Separately, the imp's **head sits a touch higher** now, reducing its overlap with the body to a clean minimal neck join (a small tunable `ImpFigure._headRaise`, applied to the imp itself so it reads the same wherever the imp appears).
- **No pop-ups / dialog boxes as chrome.** Prefer in-world, diegetic presentation over modal windows. One deliberate, minimal exception: the narration is held in a **rounded hand-drawn ink dialogue box** near the bottom of the screen that **floats clear of the bottom edge** (a tunable bottom offset, so it never hugs the edge), with softly rounded corners, a faint paper fill, and a black ink border kept alive by a slight controlled wobble (not a rough scribble and not a sterile vector rectangle). Long narration **pages by whole sentences** inside it: a page holds as many complete sentences as fit, and a sentence is never split across two boxes, advanced by click or key so it never overflows or clips. The text is **centred** in the box (horizontal centre, vertical middle) in the Caveat handwriting font, with a consistent inner margin so it never touches the border; the box is sized with a little slack (a touch larger than a tight fit) so the handwriting reads easily. The choice affordances sit outside it, never over it. It is not a UI panel, not a modal, and carries no portraits or buttons; it is just a framed caption area in the ink aesthetic. A stylised ink frame for the narrator's words is allowed; heavy chrome and modal pop-ups still are not.
- **Minimal on-screen detail.** Every UI element must justify its presence; when in doubt, remove it.
- The choice presentation itself should feel like part of the scene, not a UI overlay stapled on top.
- **Composed, centred tableaux.** Each dilemma is laid out clean and balanced in the space above the dialogue box, centred on a consistent baseline (the trolley grouped on its rail with the fork near centre; the room symmetric about the centre line) rather than scattered or drifting to one side. The Track is the layout source of truth for the trolley; the room raises or lowers to clear whichever caption (the bottom box in the opening, the top caption in the standalone scenes).
- **The trolley branch is a schematic right-angle switch, not a curve.** The main line runs straight through the fork; the second track steps down at a right angle and then runs straight and parallel below it (`---|----` over `|____`). The trolley follows it straight, corner, straight, staying level through the corner (it does not bank nose-down). Both trolleys share the track, so both read the same way. The tracks are spaced with room to read clearly: the binary branch sits a good way below the main line, and the three-track finish spreads its three rails evenly, both exposed as `[SerializeField]` so the spacing stays tunable; the figures, the trolley path, the ties, and the imp keep-out all follow from those values. The whole tableau also sits **lifted up the screen** by a tunable vertical offset, so it reads higher and leaves cleaner room below for the dialogue box and the choices (which stay put); the figures, path, ties, the dark-track shroud, and the keep-out all follow the lift, and on the three-track the option row deliberately does not (it stays in the choice area).
- **Choice affordances sit clear of the tracks, above the dialogue box.** Every dilemma's choice options (the binary text choices, and the three-track's three option labels) sit in a row in the choice-affordance area just above the dialogue box, clear of the rails and figures, never overlapping the scene. They read as part of the scene, not chrome: ink text, brighter on hover, no amber (amber stays for the tracks' own accents like the lever). The diegetic lever is still the binary trolley's primary pick; the option row is the shared, consistent affordance across binary and multiple-choice.
- **Death is the fall, not a mark.** When a figure is struck in a consequence it only folds, topples and fades as one stroke; no strike lines or impact puff are drawn over it. In the whose-life-counts second trolley the five branded wretches are drawn with **red** outlines (their whole ink line is red, marking the damned five at a glance; part of that dilemma's figure setup, see the Colour section); the one innocent on the branch stays a normal black figure, and no other figure is red.
- **Credits roll (placeholder).** The ending credits are a clean white page with the credits scrolling upward: each ROLE label in **amber** over its name in black ink, in the Caveat hand, consistent with the dialogue. Minimal and spare, no chrome; it is the one full-screen text screen and reuses the game's type and palette. See `gameplay.md` (Endings) and `tech.md`.

## Audio
- **Text-first.** Ship with text; full voice acting for the narrator is a later, high-impact upgrade (VO freezes the script, so keep writing fluid by staying text-only for now).
- Music and SFX carry mood in the meantime; keep them spare to match the visual minimalism.
- **Current placeholders.** A single looped background track plays quietly under everything (a low default level, tunable in the Inspector). The imp has a **gibberish blip voice**: as its lines type, short pitched blips fire every couple of characters (Animal Crossing / Undertale style), skipping spaces and newlines and stopping the moment a line finishes or is advanced. The clips were reworked **lower and warmer**, and the playback retuned to suit them, a **narrow pitch spread** around one (so it reads as a talky mutter, not a shrill stutter) and a blip guaranteed on the first revealed character so the voice never silently drops out; the pitch range and characters-per-blip are exposed. Both the track and the blips are placeholders, easy to swap later.
