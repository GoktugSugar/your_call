# Architecture

How the remake is built. It describes what exists in the build, and grows as systems land. Design intent lives in `PREMISE.md`; the reasons behind each choice are in `DECISIONS.md`.

## Engine

Godot 4.7, Forward+ renderer, GDScript with static typing. The base canvas is 1920x1080 (`canvas_items` stretch, `keep` aspect, paper coloured clear colour), so every position and size in the project is in those units. The window opens maximized and the canvas scales to fit.

## Layout

| Folder | What lives there |
|---|---|
| `ink/` | The ink look: the brush that turns paths into hand-drawn lines, the boil clock, the shared style and palette, the paper ground. |
| `figures/` | The reusable rigs drawn in ink: the souls (the stick figure, its poses and kinds, the baby) and the imp. |
| `doodles/` | The doodles the stages show (a would you rather's two, a shop's item), one script each, named by id (`doodles/<id>.gd`). What several doodles draw alike and is no doodle itself, such as the head in profile the head doodles share, is in `doodles/parts/`, out of the list of doodles. |
| `cinematics/` | Cinematic beats, one folder each (`cinematics/<name>/<name>_cinematic.tscn`): the opening crash and the waking that opens every later chapter, with the eyelids they share at the root. |
| `stages/` | Question presentations, one folder each. A stage stages any question that uses it, from that question's data. Controls more than one stage uses, such as the push button, are in `stages/controls/`. |
| `ui/` | The dialogue box, the choice row, the crowd reveal, the money chapter's balance, the credits, and the theme. |
| `game/` | The run: the main scene, the question flow, what the run remembers (`game/run/`), the content model and loader, the profile, the player's settings. |
| `content/` | The game's content as data. Its format is in `content/README.md`. |
| `assets/` | Imported files: fonts, music in `assets/audio/music/`, and voice clips in `assets/voice/` (stored with Git LFS). |
| `dev/` | Development-only tools and samplers. Main adds the dev tools in debug builds only, and `dev/` stays out of release exports. |
| `tests/` | GUT tests. |
| `addons/` | Third-party addons: GUT 9.7.1. |
| `docs/` | Design docs, decision log, open questions. Hidden from Godot. |
| `tools/` | Offline tooling: the voice pipeline (`tools/voice/`, Python). Hidden from Godot. |

A feature keeps its scene, script and resources together in its folder. Files and folders are `snake_case`.

## The ink look (`ink/`)

Everything on screen is ink drawn in code. Nothing is a sprite.

- **`InkBrush`** turns a densely sampled path into a line. It nudges each point off the path along its normal by two layers of smooth 1D noise: a slow drift (the unsteady hand, the same every frame) and a small fast wiggle that changes with the boil variant. The width swells and thins along the line, loose ends taper and overshoot slightly, and a closed shape runs a little past its own start, like a pen lifted late. Short lines wobble less. The result is a triangle strip drawn with `RenderingServer.canvas_item_add_triangle_array`. It also draws fills (washes under a line) and round dots (eyes), and lays out the crayon scribble that colours in a shape (`hatch_ellipse`: back and forth strokes joined into one zigzag, kept inside the edge).
- **`ink_line.gdshader`** reads the strip's UVs: `UV.y` runs across the line, so the shader softens the edges by screen-space derivatives (antialiasing without MSAA), adds a faint grain along `UV.x`, and can dissolve a drawing away in noisy blotches. Fills and dots use a negative `UV.x` to skip the grain.
- **`InkStyle`** (`ink/ink_style.tres`) holds every tunable of the line: boil rate and variant count, wobble, width, taper, overshoot, sampling. **`InkPalette`** (`ink/palette.tres`) holds the colours by meaning. Tuning either retunes the whole game.
- **`Ink`** (autoload, `ink/ink.gd`) is the boil clock. Five times a second it moves the page to its next variant (three variants by default) and every ink drawing redraws, the way hand-drawn animation redraws its lines. It also keeps a running time that idle motion reads, so breathing steps with the boil. It keeps ticking while the game is paused. A drawing can instead pass a fractional variant, and the brush then blends smoothly between two boil shapes; the imp does this for his soft waver.
- **`InkLine`** is one line ready for the brush; **`InkPainter`** paints a list of them into any canvas item, drawn on in order by a `reveal` fraction. Each line's wash goes down just before the line, so a drawing lists what is behind first. The painter keeps every line at least `min_screen_width` screen pixels wide, whatever the window size or the node's scale. It also hooks Controls up to the ink material and the boil.
- **`InkDrawing`** is the Node2D every ink drawing extends. It can carry its own `InkStyle` (the imp's line is heavier and wavers more). It draws authored `InkStroke` resources or, in subclasses, lines built in code (`_build_lines`). It can draw itself on (`draw_on`) or dissolve away (`dissolve_away`), and it keeps its line width constant however the node is scaled, as a pen would. Drawings are `@tool`, so they preview in the editor.
- **Paper** (`ink/paper/paper.tscn`) is a CanvasLayer at the back with a still shader: a fine tooth, faint clouds and fibres. It never boils.

## Figures (`figures/`)

- **`Soul`** is anything on a stage that can die. `fold()` takes it down and dissolves it, then emits `folded`; the base only dissolves, and souls with a body to fall override it. `head_top()` says where something floating over a soul goes; the base says its origin, and souls with a head say just above it.
- **`StickFigure`** is the soul almost everyone is: a jointed rig with dot eyes. Arms are forward kinematics from the shoulders; legs are two-bone IK from the hips to planted feet, so a crouch bends the knees and the feet stay put. Knees bend outward when standing and up off the ground once the figure is lying down; a figure on its knees crosses its feet over (both legs leave the same hip point, so it does not show), which rests each knee on the ground with the shin splayed out along it. It breathes, stepped with the boil. Its origin is the ground between its feet. Small switchable details set souls apart without new art: open, closed or hidden eyes, a pregnant belly, a cane, tufts of hair, a crown, a beating heart over the head, z marks drifting up from it, and a pistol in the screen right hand. The heart and the z marks move on the page clock and redraw with the boil, like breathing. `head_top()` says where something floating over the figure goes.
- **The pistol** is seen from the player: its rounded front over the hand with the dark muzzle looking out, and the grip below the hand, both in a muted ink wash so they read as solid and hide the body behind them. The pose says which way it points (`pistol_down`): pointed down at the ground, the front swings below the hand into the narrow length of the barrel, the muzzle at its end. It follows the hand through a fall. `muzzle_point()` says where the muzzle is right now, for a flash.
- **Walking.** `walk_to(x, speed)` walks a stick figure along the ground and emits `arrived`. `FigureWalk` plans the walk ahead: where each foot lands, so a planted foot never slides, and the body's pose at any moment (a dip just after each landing, arms swinging opposite the legs, a lean into the walk). The figure faces the way it goes (`facing`: front, left or right, which slides the eyes and bends the knees that way) and ends standing in its pose. Its own pace is a stride every `step_time`; a set speed quickens the steps. Big motion like this runs at full frame rate.
- **`BabyBundle`** is a soul that is not a stick figure: a swaddled bundle with a round head, lying on the rails. **`FutureBaby`** (`figures/baby/future_baby.tscn`) is the same baby with two cues kept one step removed from any real person: a small square mustache, a block of ink just under the eyes, and a paintbrush lying on the ground beside the bundle, its tip dipped in sky paint. Each is a switch with its sizes.
- **`Bear`** (`figures/bear/bear.tscn`) is the last bear: sitting up and facing the player, a rounded body, short arms whose outer edges run on from the body's outline, round feet out in front, and a round head whose ears are open bumps on its outline. Dot eyes, and a tiny smile on a lighter muzzle, both switchable. An ink outline filled solid brown, the one solid fill in the game. She breathes like the stick figures. Her fold topples her sideways round the outer edge of her feet, gathering speed, lifted wherever she would sink below the ground; she lands with a slight rock back and settles, lies still a moment, then dissolves. Her origin is the ground under her middle.
- **`SoulKind`** (`figures/kinds/<kind>.tres`) is one kind of soul as data: the scene it is made from, the palette role it is drawn in, its size, its pose and its details, whether it falls when hit (a soul already lying down only dissolves), and for a soul that starts somewhere else, such as on its knees, the steps it folds down through in place of the scene's. Content names kinds by file name: `stranger`, `guilty` (red), `partner` (pink, with the heart), `child` (smaller), `elderly` (stooped, cane, hair), `pregnant` (belly, a hand under it), `sleeper` (lying on the rail, eyes closed, z marks), `best_life` (gold, crowned), `baby`, `future_baby` (2.4 times the bundle, to read as a target among standing figures), `bear`, `armed` (aiming its pistol at the player), `dying` (on its knees, slumped toward one hand reaching out, folding from there) and `calm` (at ease, hands clasped loosely in front). `make()` builds a soul of the kind.
- **`FigurePose`** is a pose resource: hip offset, spine lean, head tilt, shoulder and elbow angles, foot offsets, and which way a pistol in the hand points. As a step in a motion it also says how the figure arrives in it (duration, curve) and how long it holds. Poses blend, mirror, and live in `figures/poses/`: `aim` (the hands meet at mid chest, elbows out, the pistol on the player) and `aim_lowered` (the same grip lowered to the waist, the pistol on the ground) for the armed, `slumped` with `slumped_fall` and `slumped_down` for the dying, `at_ease` for the calm, and for the booth `talk_aside` and `talk_chest` (a confessor's gestures while it speaks) and `verdict_guilty`, `verdict_cleared` and `verdict_shrug` (how it takes its verdict).
- **The walking button** (`figures/walking_button/`, `WalkingButton`) is the green button of 2.11 that comes alive, a running gag built to be reused. Its body is a `PushButton`, so dormant it is just the button the player presses; `ButtonLimbs` draws its stick legs (IK to little feet) and arms behind the base, and `ButtonFace` its dot eyes on the cap. `wake()` grows the limbs while it winces, pops its eyes and jolts it off the ground; `stand_up()` gets it up on its legs (`come_alive()` does both); `say(text, voice)` floats ink words over it (a `Plea`) that go where it goes, said aloud when given a voice clip, and `finish_saying()` waits for them; `hold_arms(pose)` takes a `ButtonArmPose` from `figures/walking_button/arms/` (rest, fist, hips, shrug, startled, flinch); `walk_to(x)` and `walk_off(direction)` waddle it in planted steps; `alive = true` snaps it to standing for a stage that brings it in already alive. Standing about, it bobs, blinks and taps a foot, stepped with the boil.
- **The fold** is the shared death: `fold()` plays `buckle`, `collapse` and `down`, then dissolves the figure. Falling the other way mirrors the poses. A kind can name its own steps instead, as the dying soul does from its knees, and the bear has a fall of her own.
- **The imp** is `Imp` (behaviour) over `ImpDrawing` (art). The drawing is a crude, angular little devil in his amber tint: long thin horns, a lumpy head, sly slanted eyes over a wide grin, a boxy body, floating bent arms and a crooked tail with a square tip. He has four faces: grin, smug, glare, surprised. Shot (`round_on_camera`), he is knocked back and forth, then glares and grows as if coming at the camera, and stays so, not hopping, until he next appears. His line is his own style (`figures/imp/imp_ink_style.tres`). The behaviour scribbles him into being with a pop and keeps him softly alive: his line melts between shapes, he drifts on smooth noise, bobs, sways, breathes and sways his tail, and every few seconds he fidgets with an eased shrug, tail flick, bounce or cocked head. He nods and gestures while he talks, and hops to a fresh spot for each page. While a stage is up he keeps to his wing, never onto the stage. The run player tells him when the page is his (`free_to_roam`, while he narrates with no stage up): then, at a new line (`hop_for_line`), he may step forward from his wing to the middle, and once out wander on now and then, to the middle or anywhere on the open floor, staying a while between wanders; his hops on the spot keep near where he wandered. A long trip is a run of hops (`travel_to`, a stride at a time), and before each question's stage the run player has him hop home (`go_home`). All of it is on his Roaming exports. **`ImpMouth`** moves his mouth while he talks: it reads what the dialogue box says is being spoken (`spoken_character()`), four times across each mouth shape, and opens the mouth for vowels, closes it for m, b and p and shuts it in pauses, changing shape 12 times a second; each face draws its own open mouth (`mouth_open` on `ImpDrawing`), an outline blank inside. Main hands him the speech with `set_speech`.

## Stages (`stages/`)

A **`Stage`** is a question's presentation. The question player drives every stage through the same calls: `setup(question)`, `enter()`, `set_choosing(active)`, `highlight_option(index)`, `play_consequence(index)` and `leave()`. A stage reports its in-scene controls through `option_hovered` and `option_committed`, and `option_point(index)` says where on the canvas an option can be picked in the scene, if it can. By default `enter` draws every ink drawing on and `leave` dissolves them. Stage scenes live at `stages/<type>/<type>_stage.tscn`, where `<type>` is the question's `stage.type`.

A stage can do more, each with a default that does nothing:
- **Read and change what the run remembers.** The question player hands every stage the run state (`run`), as it hands it the dialogue box: the profile, the money chapter's wallet, the kill tally, and facts later beats look back on. A stage on its own, as in a sampler, has a fresh one. Every stage that kills counts it: the tracks, the gamble, the folded strangers of the offer, a nuked country's people, and every shot.
- **Do something to the imp.** The question player hands every stage the imp (`imp`), for the gun that can be aimed at him.
- **Report how the consequence came out.** `play_consequence` returns an outcome, such as `lives` or a ladder's amount, which picks the imp's reaction, the option's profile nudge and any secret ending.
- **Bring the offer round again.** `another_round()` keeps an offer ladder going after each acceptance.
- **Take the choice by itself.** A stage that `chooses_alone()` has no choice row; the question player awaits its `choose()`.
- **Fill placeholders.** `fills()` and `fill_keys()` give the values its question's lines are filled with right now, and `fill(lines)` fills them; `option_labels()` fills the choice row's words the same way, and a stage whose options change between rounds, like an auction's rising bid, gives its own. A static `fill_variants(question)` on the stage's script lists every filling its lines can take, and `const FILLS` names the placeholders (or a static `fill_names()`, for a stage that extends another); `StageFills` reads them for the tests and the voice tools.
- **Pick how the question is asked, or find nothing to choose.** `ask_key()` names one of the question's keyed asks, such as the buyback's `nothing` when the player owns nothing; `offers_choice()` false makes the question only its ask, with no choice, consequence or reveal.
- **Make its own reveal.** `reveal_split(pick)` returns a `RevealSplit`, by default the question's own.
- **Talk.** The question player hands every stage the dialogue box (`dialogue`), for a stage whose imp speaks at moments of its own.
- **Say lines of the ask itself.** `speaks(line)` says whether someone on the stage says a line of the ask or a reaction themselves, floating over them, and `say_line(line)` has them say it; the question player closes the box for it, as the walking button confesses in the booth.
- **Cue the imp's face.** `imp_face_cued(face)` changes his face at a moment of the stage's own, such as his surprise the moment the walking button yelps; the question player passes it up and Main sets it, and it holds until his next line sets his face.

**The pick loop** (`stages/controls/pick_loop.gd`, `PickLoop`) is the amber loop a hand draws round a pick, a little over one turn of a tilted ellipse that widens as it goes; `circle(at, seconds)` draws it on. The shop circles a purchase with it.

**The push button** (`stages/controls/push_button.tscn`, `PushButton`) is the in-scene control most of Chapter 2 is pressed with: a round cap coloured in with a crayon scribble inside its pen line, sitting in a base. Its `ButtonLook` (`stages/controls/looks/<look>.tres`) sets the cap's colour and its mark; content names looks by file name: `plain`, `blue` (a ring), `red` (a dot), `green` and `gold` (a dollar sign), so the blue and red buttons never depend on colour alone. While it is live, pointing lifts the cap and clicking emits `clicked`; `highlighted` lifts it from the choice row; `press()` sinks the cap behind the front of the base and springs it back. It can carry a word on its cap (`text`), which goes on and off with the drawing.

**The trolley stage** builds itself from `stage.tracks`, a list of tracks each with its souls, named by kind. It shows the `TrackLayout` with as many routes as there are tracks:

- **Two tracks make a fork.** Track 0 runs straight on and track 1 branches off at the split. The lever stands for the first option whose outcome leaves track 0. Pulling throws the lever, then the trolley rolls along the approach and down the chosen track.
- **Three make a fan**, read top to bottom as first, second and third, with no lever. Pointing at a route lights it amber and clicking picks it; the choice row lights the same route.

The trolley speeds up all the way. Each soul folds as the trolley's front reaches it, and the trolley dissolves off the end of the line. The data can also ask for:

- **A plea** (`{"kind": ..., "says": ...}` in place of a kind): the soul holds its hands clasped, and a `Plea` inks the words on over its head once the stage is drawn. They bob with the boil and fade when the soul dies or the stage goes.
- **`roped`**: a `Rope` ties the track's souls together at the waist. It goes when the first of them is hit.
- **`"facing": "away"`**: the track's souls have no eyes, turned from the player.
- **`dark`**: a `Darkness`, a heavy ink wash with a scribbled edge, hides the track from the middle on. A trolley sent into it stops there unseen, and the dark lifts once the trolley has rolled.
- **`rolling`** (on the stage): the trolley creeps toward the split while the player decides, slowing as it nears so it never arrives on its own.

`Track` draws two rails and sleepers along a smooth path and answers positions by distance along it. `TrolleyCart` is the tram, filled with paper so it hides what it passes. `Lever` lifts when pointed at and throws when clicked.

**The doodles stage** is the quick would you rather: two small ink doodles side by side, each over its option's label, with a muted "or" between them. `stage.doodles` names them, left then right, and `DoodlesStage.make_doodle(id)` builds each from its script in `doodles/`. Pointing at a doodle lifts it and lights its label, and clicking it picks it; the choice row lifts the same doodle. On the pick an amber loop draws itself around the chosen doodle while the other one, its label and the "or" dissolve, and the pick hops once. A doodle is an `InkDrawing` built in code, standing on its origin.

**The matchup stage** is Who Would Win: the doodles stage with "vs" between the sides. A side (`stage.sides`) names a doodle in a palette role; with a `count` over one it is a crowd of small copies (`DoodleGroup.crowd`: up to a dozen in staggered rows, the back rows behind), with the count in its label. A side can be the walking button itself, squared up with its fists, or everyone the player has killed in the run as one red mob, the last bear among them if she was shot, its count filled into the label (`{kills}`). A `DoodleGroup` stands, lifts, hops and dissolves as one doodle would. The bar is voted in each side's colour, and the stage can pick the question's ask by what the run remembers (`asks`).

**The gamble stage** is the coordination gamble: the soul at stake standing apart, and a push button per option, its look named by the option's `outcome.button`. The room has voted already: its share for the gamble's button is the gamble option's crowd, and `stage.random` is the share of the room pressing at random, drawn again every play. Pressing the gamble's button makes the soul glance about while the room is counted; it lives if the room's share passed `stage.line`, and folds if not. The other button keeps it alive. A soul that lives walks out of the room, fading as it goes. Outcomes `lives` and `dies`; the reveal shows the room's vote in the buttons' colours with the line marked.

**The offer stage** is the escalating offer, a ladder: one button, the amount on offer over it, and readouts under it. `stage.tiers` lists each tier's amount and the share who took it. Taking an offer presses the button (and in 2.03 a stranger inks itself in off to the side and folds) and brings the offer back at the next tier; a tier that `repeats` comes back at the same amount. Stopping ends it, and the reveal shows the split at the tier the player stopped on. Outcomes: the amount taken, `again` for a repeated tier, and `high` or `low` on stopping when `stage.high_from` is set.

**The timer stage** takes the choice by itself: one button with a word on it and a clock counting down over it. The imp's `countdown` lines drop in at `stage.cues` without waiting for clicks, and the player presses the button (or key 1) or lets the clock run out.

**The button stage** is one button alone. The option whose outcome has `press` presses it. With `comes_alive`, the button is a dormant walking button: pressed, it yelps the stage's first line as the press lands and wakes, says the second with a fist shaken at the player, gets up and stomps off saying the third.

**The better offer stage** is a button and the best life: the golden soul inks itself in on the ask line `stage.cue`. Taking it presses the button, and the soul hops for joy and strolls off, fading; keeping your own, it turns its back and fades.

**The nuke stage** is a red button that cures cancer at the price of one random country from `content/countries.json`. Pressed, a hand-drawn world map (`WorldMap`: every landmass a loop of longitude and latitude, projected flat and smoothed) inks itself in, a red pin (`MapPin`) goes round and round over it while the imp says his `spin` lines, then drops onto the country; its name, in amber with a halo of paper, and its people ink on beside it, clear of where a mushroom cloud (`MushroomCloud`) doodles onto the spot. Outcomes, in order: `home` when the pin lands on the player's own country (from the system's region), the country's code when there is a reaction for it, or the key of its band by people (`stage.bands`). It fills `{country}` and `{people}`.

**The shop stage** is the money chapter's buy or pass: one thing for sale, its doodle (`stage.item`) beside a hand drawn amber price tag (`PriceTag`, a card cut to a point with a hole punched in it, sized to its words). Pointing at the thing lifts it and lights the buying option, and clicking buys. Buying pays out of the run's wallet: the pick loop circles the thing, it hops, and the balance counts down; keeping the money, or trying without enough, fades the offer, and the tag shakes its head at an empty wallet. A price of `everything` asks for the whole balance. With `step` the thing is bought one at a time, each dearer by the step, coming round again like an offer ladder, with the tag and a tally running beside it. Outcomes: `bought`, `broke` (which counts as keeping the money in the reveal), `passed`; one at a time, `first`, `more`, `ran_out`, `broke`, and on stopping `some` or `none`. It fills `{price}` and `{count}`.

**The auction stage** extends the shop with a rival: trying to buy brings the walking button waddling in from the side away from the imp, already alive, to bid a step over the player; the tag climbs with every bid, the choice row's words become "Bid $40" and "Drop out", and no money moves. Dropping out lets the button take the thing and walk off with it; the bid that reaches `persist` fires the question's ending, after the imp cuts in (`stop`, moving on by itself) and the button gloats and goes. The button's lines are the question's `arrive`, `taunts`, `won` and `gloat` sets. Outcomes `auction`, `bid`, `cursed`, `dropped`, `passed`, `broke`; it fills `{price}`, `{rival}` and `{bid}`, and remembers that the button was in the auction.

**The buyback stage** lays out everything the wallet owns in rows (doodle, name, the offer in amber), dimmed, and lights them one at a time in the order they were bought; selling fades the row and pays the offer back into the wallet, keeping dims it. It picks its ask: `nothing` (no choice at all), the first thing's question id when the question has an ask by that name (End Poverty alone), or `items`. Outcomes: the thing's own id when the option has a reaction for it, `last` or `none` on the last row, otherwise `sold` and `kept` the first time and `again` after.

**The gun stage** is the gun chapter, first person: up to five targets (`stage.targets`: a soul kind, or a doodle for a thing that is not a soul, at a place on the ground, scaled up from the tracks' size since the gun brings them close) and no gun on screen. An ink sight (`Crosshair`) follows the pointer while the player chooses, and amber brackets (`LockBrackets`) close in round whatever it is over, from the drawn bounds of the target's lines; a click fires at it. The choice row offers the same shots in words and lowering the gun; a shot picked there moves the sight onto its target first. A shot is heard (the gunshot on the SFX bus, its pitch varied a little), flashes the page, jolts the view, and kicks the sight, which fades away (`fired` is emitted as it goes off), and the target folds, counted in the run's kill tally; with `survivors: leave` the stick figures left standing walk off. Lowering drops the sight away. An option's outcome names what it fires at: a target's id, `any` (the one clicked, or one at random from the row: firing blind), `imp`, or `self`, or `hold`. The variants are stage data: the imp as a hidden target (a hidden option, picked only on the stage; the shot at him lands harder: the sight is gone at once, ink bursts where he is hit, the world holds still for a heartbeat, the view jolts hard, and he is squashed, knocked about and rounds on the camera, and the question's ending fires), a doodle that charges at the player while they choose and turns out to be the walking button, shot or not (`charge`; shot, it stops where it was hit, its words kept readable however small it is), nothing asked and no choice row, the gun lowering itself when time runs out (`alone`), an armed target that fires back at a lowered gun half the time, its muzzle flashing (`fires_back`, `MuzzleFlash`), an empty gun's dry click (`empty`), the gun turned on the player, the brackets framing the whole view (`self`), and a target that dies anyway once the gun is lowered (`dies_anyway`). A breakdown by target (`crowd` and `label` on each, coloured by its `role`) makes the reveal one bar per target plus holding. Pleas float over the targets that have `says`.

**The receipt stage** is a tableau: a paper slip (`ReceiptSlip`, a torn bottom edge) draws in under the balance and prints itself a line at a time: what is still owned with what it cost, the dearest few when there are many, what the player started with, spent and has left, and beside it, faint, the doodles of the dearest things passed on. It reports `hoard`, `spend` or `middle` by the share of the pot left, and nudges the profile by that share.

**The booth stage** is the confession booth of Chapter 7: `ConfessionBooth`, a tall booth seen from the front, open so the confessor stands in full view, with a peaked roof and a knob, an arched opening, a curtain gathered back to one side in a muted wash, and a step. It is drawn on for each case and dissolves before the reveal, like every stage. Confessors (`stage.confessors`) file in from the left, inking in as they walk, and the words they stand by float over the roof; two stand either side of the booth, coming in from either side, their words over their heads. While a confessor's line is up in the dialogue box (its `voice` matching the confessor's), that confessor moves through its talking gestures (`talk_aside`, `talk_chest`) and settles when it stops. The verdict is the gavel, heard and not seen as the gun is: its knock on the SFX bus and a jolt of the page; each confessor takes the verdict meant for it (`guilty`: it hangs its head and shuts its eyes; `cleared`: it opens its arms with a hop of relief; `everyone`: it shrugs), held longer the heavier the case. Once the imp has had his say, the stage's `leave` files them out, fading as they go, kept clear of the imp's wing (`walk_bounds`), and the booth dissolves. The walking button can confess: it slides in as a plain button, comes alive in the booth, and says its lines over the roof (`WalkingButton.words_spot`) in its own voice.

## Cinematics (`cinematics/`)

A cinematic is a CanvasLayer over everything with a `play()` the run player awaits. An advance press skips it, but not in its first moments, so the click that focuses the window does not throw it away.

- **The crash** (`cinematics/crash/`) opens the game. `CrashView` is the memory in pale ink on black: a night road, its centre line streaming by, and two headlights bearing down, their glow on a canvas item of its own without the ink material so it falls off smoothly. `CrashCinematic` flickers it at a low frame rate with frames lost to black, swerves the view, lands the hit with a white flash and a shake (emitting `hit`, for its sound), cuts to black, then wakes.
- **The eyelids** (`cinematics/eyelids.tscn`, `Eyelids`) are the waking both cinematics share: `eyelids.gdshader`, on a ColorRect, clears the dark in uneven blotches that blink, drift and sharpen as the eyes focus (`open()`), or closes it in the same way run backwards, drooping, fluttering and shutting (`close()`).
- **The waking** (`cinematics/waking/`) opens every chapter after the first: the eyes close on the empty page, the dark holds, and they open again. It sits over the dialogue box and under the credits.

## UI (`ui/`)

- **`DialogueBox`**: the hand-drawn `InkFrame` (gone over twice, on box paper) sized to its text and growing upward from just above the bottom edge. `Pager` splits each line into pages of whole sentences, as few as fit in three lines and as even as possible, closing and reopening a colour tag across a break. Text inks on through `InkOnEffect`, a rich text effect that fades characters in by a schedule: a steady pace with breaths after punctuation. The page is laid out in full first, so nothing shifts. A click or key finishes the page, then moves on. The schedule is the hook for voice: it can come from audio timings instead. `spoken_character()` reads the same schedule on the same clock, so it says which character is being said even after a click has finished showing the page; the imp's mouth follows it. `say(lines, hold_last, auto)` can move each page on by itself once it has been said and has stayed up for `auto_hold`, and every page does when the player has auto skip on (`PlayerSettings.auto_advance`), except the page a choice follows, which is held for the choice anyway. The setting is watched while a page waits, so turning it on takes the page along. `interject(line)` throws a line's first page up at once, in place of whatever is there, without waiting or taking clicks, the way the imp needles over the timer.
- **`ChoiceRow`**: the options as muted hand-lettered `ChoiceWord`s above the box. Pointing lifts a word to amber and draws an `InkUnderline`; picking keeps the pick amber and fades the rest. Mouse or number keys. A stage control (the lever, a button) lights and picks options through the same calls.
- **`CrowdReveal`**: the lowercase muted heading, then a `CrowdRow` per option, each a label over a `CrowdBar` that inks in from the left while its bare number counts up past its end. The pick is amber, the crowd darker ink. While it is up it is the only thing listening: the first input finishes the inking, the next dismisses it. It shows a `RevealSplit`: the heading, labels, shares and pick, and optionally meaning colours for the bars (the pick's label still goes amber) and a `CrowdMark`, an ink line across the bars at the share a coordination gamble's crowd had to pass, with its name over it.
- **`BalanceReadout`**: the money chapter's running balance at the top of the screen, a quiet "balance" over the amount in amber. Main hands it each run's state; it inks in when a chapter opens a wallet, counts to every change, and fades when the wallet is put away or an ending fires.
- **`Credits`**: a clean paper page over everything; the title and each credit (the role in amber over the name in ink) scroll up from `content/credits.json`, and the page fades. A press hurries them along.
- **Theme** (`ui/theme/ink_theme.tres`, set as the project theme): Caveat at weight 600 (`caveat_semibold.tres`), 44 px, warm ink colour.

## The question loop (`game/`)

**`QuestionPlayer.play(question, run)`** is the core loop as one chain of awaits:

1. The stage is built from the question's data and draws itself on.
2. The imp asks: the dialogue box says the ask and leaves its last page up. Placeholders are filled by the stage. A line someone on the stage says themselves floats over them while the box is away (`Stage.speaks`).
3. The choice row offers the options above the box while the stage's controls are live (all but hidden ones, which only the stage can pick; picking one there ends the row's offer unpicked). The lever, a button or a doodle and the row light and pick in sync. A stage that takes the choice by itself (the timer) does so with no row.
4. The box clears and the stage plays the consequence, which reports how it came out.
5. The imp reacts over the world to that outcome, page by page. The click on the last page is the gate.
6. The profile takes the option's silent nudges for that outcome. On an offer ladder the offer comes round again (back to 3), with the imp's line for the round held up while the player chooses again (`round_lines`: hold, auto or click).
7. The reaction clears, the stage dissolves, and the crowd reveal shows the stage's split until the player dismisses it. When the pick was the least popular and the question has `least_popular` lines, the imp says them first.

A question whose stage finds nothing to choose is only its ask. A **tableau**, a question with no options such as the receipt, is its stage drawing on, its ask if it has one, the consequence, and the question's lines named by the outcome, with the click on the last page the gate; no reveal.

`phase_changed` reports each step and `choosing_started` each choice, for tools. When an outcome fires a secret ending, the loop stops after the reaction, returns the ending, and leaves the stage up for the run player to take down.

**`RunPlayer.play_run(chapters, run, from_chapter, from_beat)`** plays the chapters in order, and **`play_chapter`** a chapter's beats in order, from the top or from a named beat. A cinematic beat plays its scene (`cinematics/<name>/<name>_cinematic.tscn`, awaited through `play()`) over everything. A narration beat has the imp say its lines, and a question beat goes to the question player; the imp scribbles himself in before the first beat that needs him, and takes his leave at the end of each chapter. A secret ending ends the run: a beat's pause, then the stage dissolves, the imp fades and the credits roll (`ui/credits/credits.tscn`, awaited through `roll()`). A scene that does not exist yet is skipped with a warning, so the run never stalls on it. A chapter with a `wallet` opens it on the run state as its first words are said and puts it away at its end, or when an ending fires.

**`Main`** loads the run (`content/run.json`: the chapters in order), wires the imp to the dialogue box (a hop per page, a chance to roam at each new line, a nod while text inks on, the line's face or else his grin, all only on his own pages: while a confessor speaks in the box he keeps still and his mouth shut), and for now plays the run from the top over and over, with a fresh run state each run, which it hands the balance readout.

**`RunState`** (`game/run/`) is what one run remembers from beat to beat and chapter to chapter: the silent `profile`, the money chapter's **`Wallet`** while it is open (the pot it started with, the balance, every **`Purchase`** in the order it was bought, and what was passed on; buying adds to a thing already owned, and selling it back forgets it), a **`KillTally`** of everything killed by kind, and facts later beats look back on, such as the walking button having woken in 2.11, bid in the auction, or been shot or spared in 4.05 (`remember`, `remembers`). A run started part way, from the dev menu, first takes what each skipped chapter would have left behind (`assume`, from the chapter's `if_skipped`). Nothing saves it yet; the save is still to be built.

**`Profile`** is the silent portrait: a signed leaning per axis, never shown during play. The axes come from `content/profile_axes.json`. An option's nudge can depend on how its consequence came out (`OptionData.profile_for`), such as no nudge at all for a bid in the auction.

## Content (`content/`, `game/content/`)

Content is JSON, one file per chapter, read by **`ContentLoader`** into `ChapterData`, `CinematicData`, `QuestionData` (a tableau is one with no options), `NarrationData`, `OptionData` and `LineData`. The loader never stops on a problem: it keeps what it can and lists each problem with where it is, and the tests fail on any. Every line gets a stable id from where it sits, such as `ch1.lever.pull.1`; a line can carry a voice direction for the voice tools. Words a soul says on the stage are collected into the question's stage lines (`ch1.begging.stage.1`), so they are checked and exported like any other line; readouts and captions are collected as stage texts. `all_lines()` on any beat lists every line it can show. **`Markup`** handles the colour tags: stripping them, turning them into palette colours, and checking them; it also finds placeholders and checks for dashes and stray braces. **`Numbers`** writes money and big counts the way the player reads them ("$12,500").

A reaction, a profile nudge or an ending can be keyed by the outcome the stage reports (`OptionData.reaction_for`, `profile_for`, `ending_for`), an ask can be keyed by a name the stage picks (`QuestionData.ask_for`), and a question can hold named sets of lines its stage says (`lines_in`). A line's placeholders are filled by `LineData.filled(fills, keys)`, whose copy's id ends with the keys of the values it used, so a filling has the same id wherever it is made. The format is documented in `content/README.md`.

## Voice (`tools/voice/`, `game/voice/`)

Lines are voiced with ElevenLabs. Each line is one take, so its delivery flows across its sentences, and the game plays a page at a time out of it.

- **Casting.** `design_auditions.py --speaker <speaker>` designs candidate voices from written descriptions into `voice_auditions/` (ignored by git and Godot), every candidate for a speaker reading the same lines; each design comes back as three takes, and all three are kept. `--dry-run` says what a round would spend first. `save_voice.py <speaker> <slug>` saves the chosen take as a permanent ElevenLabs voice and records it per speaker in `tools/voice/voices.json`, with the TTS model and settings.
- **Voicing.** `voice_lines.py` asks Godot for every line (`dev/export_voice_lines.gd`: id, speaker, the voice that says it, spoken text, SHA-1; a line with placeholders once for every filling its stage lists, each under its own id), then voices each line whose voice is in `voices.json` and whose clip is missing or out of date. A line's voice is its speaker's, such as `imp`, or for a speaker with several, such as the confessors, the speaker's and its own (`confessor.recliner`). `design_auditions.py --speaker confessor` casts every confessor at once. It uses the with-timestamps endpoint, so every clip comes with the time each character is spoken. A clip is out of date when its text or its voice settings change. Clips go to `assets/voice/<speaker>/<line id>.mp3`, timings beside them as `.json`, and `assets/voice/manifest.json` records the text hash and voice signature of each.
- **Playing.** `VoiceLibrary` looks a line's clip up by id and only returns it while the line's spoken text still hashes to what the clip was made from; recolouring a line keeps its voice, rewording it silences it until it is voiced again. `VoiceClip` gives the stretch of the take for a page's characters and when each character starts. `DialogueBox` plays that stretch on the Voice bus and inks the page on in time with it, reading the audio clock. The first click finishes the page while the voice plays on; the next moves on, fading the voice out on one of two players so it never cuts off the next take. A line kept silent (`"voiced": false`) has no clip. Floating words are voiced the same way: given a clip, a `Plea` plays it on the Voice bus and inks on in time with it, and counts as said once the take has played out. The walking button says its lines in its own clips and finishes each before it moves on.
- **The key** is read from `.env` or `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` and never printed.

## Audio (`game/audio/`, `default_bus_layout.tres`)

Buses: Master, with Music, SFX and Voice under it. Sound effects are made with ElevenLabs: `tools/sfx/sound_takes.py` makes a batch of takes of a sound described in `tools/sfx/sounds.json` into `sfx_auditions/` (git ignores it), and saves the one picked by ear into `assets/sfx/`, through Git LFS. The gunshot is the first. The Voice bus starts with a pitch shift, which the dialogue box sets so a voice played faster keeps its pitch (`voice_speed`). The `Music` autoload loops one quiet track under everything, carried across scene changes, fading it in and dipping it while someone talks. A missing track is silence.

## Development tools (`dev/`)

All of these run from the command line; see CLAUDE.md for the exact commands.

- `check_scripts.gd` loads every script and fails if any does not compile (the headless import alone does not catch parse errors).
- `dev_tools.tscn` (debug builds only): F2 opens `DevMenu`, a list of every chapter in the run with the picked chapter's questions beside it, to jump to (it opens on the chapter being played, a long chapter's questions come in even columns, and the game holds still while it is open; a jump restarts the run there, and F5 then restarts at the last jump), with a switch for auto skip over it until the settings screen has one, F5 restarts, F3 shows the chapter, the phase and the profile. On the command line it can force a window size, start the run at a named chapter and beat, autoplay a question (pick an option in the choice row, or point at it on the stage and click it there with synthetic pointer events: the lever, a track, a doodle, a button; a list of picks for an offer that comes round again; on a stage that takes the choice by itself, click there or leave it be), speed the game up, save a screenshot before each click, a burst during the consequence and one every few seconds throughout, and quit after a number of questions.
- `capture_runner.tscn` runs any scene and saves screenshots at set times.
- `export_voice_lines.gd` writes every line's id, speaker, spoken text and hash for the voice tools.
- `ink_sampler`, `figure_sampler` and `imp_sampler` show the line, the poses and fold, and the imp's faces talking along with a voiced line, large and at game size, beside a live imp. `soul_sampler` shows every soul kind side by side in rows, with a plea, a roped pair, a soul turned away, a patch of the dark, a soul walking back and forth and the armed soul with its pistol lowered; `--fold=<kind>,...` (or `all`) folds those kinds after a second, to the left with `--fold_left`. `doodle_sampler` shows every doodle a page at a time (`--page`, `--ids`, and `--scale` to see them small) and previews the doodles stage; `money_sampler` stages the receipt, the buyback and the stranger's years from an already spent wallet; `button_sampler` shows every push button look, lifted, half pressed and with a word; `map_sampler` shows the world map with a pin in every country (one pin and its cloud with `--pin`); `walking_button_sampler` presses the green button, brings it to life and walks it off beside its arm poses; `cinematic_sampler` plays the crash, the waking (alone with `--waking`) and the credits.
