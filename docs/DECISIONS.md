# Decision log

A dated record of every decision, newest at the bottom. Append an entry whenever a decision is made or changes, and update the doc it touches. Each entry says what was decided, who decided it (the user for creative calls, the dev for technical ones) and why.

## 2026-10-21: the remake starts

- **Engine and language: Godot 4.7, Forward+, GDScript.** User. The editor is the 4.7.2 standard build. GDScript matches the user's other Godot projects and needs no .NET toolchain or build step.
- **The ink look is drawn in code.** User, on the dev's recommendation. Every line on screen, from figures and the imp to doodles and UI frames, is generated at runtime as a hand-drawn ink stroke that boils. Figures are jointed rigs reposed by their joint angles. There is no sprite art pipeline, so nothing waits on drawing. How the strokes are made is in `ARCHITECTURE.md`.
- **Voice acting comes right after the first slice.** User. The slice is built ready for voice: lines are addressable, colour tags are stripped before anything reaches TTS, and the dialogue box can take its typing pace from audio.
- **No Godot MCP server.** User. This supersedes the handoff README's suggestion to set one up again. The dev runs, tests and captures the game through the Godot command line instead.
- **Repository.** Dev. The project folder the user created in the Godot 4.7 project manager became the clone of github.com/GoktugSugar/your_call, on top of its placeholder commit. The Unity backup bundle and the handoff folder sit beside the project but stay out of git (listed in `.git/info/exclude`).
- **The handoff pack became living docs.** Dev. `PREMISE.md` and `SCRIPT.md` moved into `docs/` and now stay in sync with the build. The open questions in PREMISE section 16, and the points sections 9 and 14 leave to the user, moved to `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`.
- **Git workflow.** Dev, keeping the user's old rules. `main` stays runnable. The project setup went straight onto `main` as the base. Every feature after it goes on a `feat/<short-description>` branch with a pull request, merged once the user signs off at the check-in.
- **Display.** Dev. Base resolution 1920x1080 with stretch mode `canvas_items` and aspect `keep`. The clear colour is the paper colour, so on other aspect ratios the letterbox bars read as more paper. The window opens at 1600x900 during development.
- **Type: Caveat SemiBold.** Dev, keeping PREMISE's default. It is the variable font from Google Fonts set to weight 600, downloaded fresh under the SIL Open Font License, with the licence beside it.
- **Input.** Dev. Everything goes through Input Map actions: `advance` (left click, Space, Enter), `choose_1` to `choose_6` (number keys), `pause` (Esc), plus `dev_restart` (F5) and `dev_overlay` (F3), which only act in development builds.
- **Project layout by feature.** Dev. Folders are `ink/`, `figures/`, `doodles/`, `stages/`, `ui/`, `game/` and `content/`, with `docs/` and `tools/` hidden from Godot. Details are in `ARCHITECTURE.md`.
- **CLAUDE.md carries a GodotPrompter section.** User. Agents, subagents included, must check for a matching `godot-prompter` skill before building a Godot system.

## 2026-10-22: the first slice, 1.02 The Lever

All dev decisions, made while building the slice. The look and pacing numbers are starting points to tune with the user.

- **Content is JSON, one file per chapter.** Lines are plain strings, or objects when they need a speaker or a face. JSON is plain text that diffs cleanly and that the voice tooling can read without a parser of its own. `ContentLoader` reports every malformed or unknown field with its location, and the content tests fail on any problem. The format is in `content/README.md`.
- **Line ids come from where a line sits** (`ch1.lever.pull.1`), so nobody types or maintains them. The voice pipeline will also track each clip by its text, so a copy change marks exactly that clip for regeneration.
- **How the ink is drawn.** Every line is a triangle strip built in GDScript and drawn through `RenderingServer`, with a shader that softens edges from screen-space derivatives. Wobble and boil come from cheap 1D value noise. The whole page boils together on one clock: 5 fps, 3 variants. Static drawings only redraw on the boil; moving ones redraw every frame.
- **Idle motion steps like hand-drawn animation.** Figures breathe in step with the boil, and the imp idles at 12 fps. Big motion, such as the trolley, a fall or a hop, runs at full frame rate.
- **The stick figure rig.** Arms use forward kinematics and legs use two-bone IK to planted feet, all from pose resources that blend and mirror. Death is the shared fold: buckle, collapse, down, then an ink dissolve. There are no strike marks or impact effects.
- **The imp is drawn in code too**, with four faces (grin, smug, glare, surprised). He uses the palette's `imp` tint, the last build's amber, until the open question is settled; one palette change switches him to black ink. He keeps to one wing for a whole question.
- **Stages are data-driven scenes behind one contract.** A question's `stage.type` picks `stages/<type>/<type>_stage.tscn`, and the stage builds itself from the question's data. The trolley stage handles any number of tracks and figures, and the lever stands for the option that leaves the straight track.
- **The loop is one chain of awaits** in `QuestionPlayer`. Each component listens for input only while the loop is waiting on it, so the reaction and the reveal never share a click.
- **UX defaults in place, to confirm at tuning.** A click while text is inking finishes the page. The tableau dissolves before the crowd reveal, so the reveal stands alone. Consequences cannot be skipped. Pages hold up to 3 lines, and text inks on at 42 characters a second with short breaths after punctuation.
- **New input action `pick`** (left click) for pointing at things on the stage and in the choice row, separate from `advance`.
- **Testing with GUT 9.7.1**, the version the user already runs on Godot 4.7, vendored in `addons/gut`.
- **Development tools are debug only.** Autoplay, screenshots, the overlay and F5 restart live in `dev/` and are added by `Main` only in debug builds. `dev/` will be excluded from release exports when the export presets are set up.

## 2026-10-22: first tuning round on the slice

- **Settled from the slice's open questions.** User. The imp stays amber. A click while text is inking finishes the page. The tableau dissolves before the crowd reveal. Consequences always play in full. The souls stand on the rails.
- **The imp is redrawn after the old build's imp.** User, from a picture of the old imp. The clean smiley read wrong, so he is now crude and angular: long thin horns, a lumpy head, sly slanted eyes over a wide grin, a boxy body, floating bent arms, and a crooked tail with a square tip. His line is heavier than the rest of the page.
- **The imp's jitter is soft, never sharp.** User. Random twitching read like an animatronic, so all his idle motion is now eased and continuous. His line melts smoothly from one shape to the next (dev: the brush can blend between boil variants). He drifts on smooth noise, and his fidgets (a shrug, a tail flick, a bounce, a cocked head) ease in and out. The rest of the page keeps its stepped 5 fps boil.
- **Lines are heavier and never too thin on screen.** User reported thin lines in the 1600x900 window. Dev: the base line is 5.2 px (was 4.4), every line keeps at least 3 screen pixels at any window size, and a taper takes at most a fifth of a line, so short marks like the imp's eyes stay bold. The imp's eyes and mouth are drawn heavier still.
- **The game opens maximized.** Dev. The 1600x900 window drew everything at 83 percent. The `--window=WxH` dev switch forces an exact size for checking.
- **Stage tuning.** User. The stick figures are a fifth smaller, the lever sits lower, and the trolley's wheels tuck up under its body.
- **Washes paint in line order.** Dev. Each line's wash goes down just before the line itself, so a drawing lists what is behind first. The trolley's body now hides the tops of its wheels.
- **The imp, second pass.** User. His grin is finer than his eyes, and both arms join his body at the shoulders. His body no longer swells with each breath and barely bobs, and the bounce fidget is gone, because together they read as straining. A lean toward the stage takes the bounce's place among his fidgets.
- **The imp's jitter is inky again, and the souls' arms are relaxed.** User. The smooth melting waver read as wobbly and bubbly, so his line boils in hand-drawn steps again, at 8 shapes a second (the page boils at 5), while his body still moves eased with no drift or random twitch. The smooth waver stays available as a switch on his drawing. His grin sits a little lower. The souls' arms open to 36 degrees: at 24 they looked like penguins, at 48 too wide.

## 2026-10-22: the voice pipeline starts

- **Voices are cast with ElevenLabs Voice Design.** Dev. `tools/voice/design_auditions.py` designs candidate voices from written descriptions, each reading the same lines, and saves the previews to `voice_auditions/` (ignored by git and Godot) with an index of their descriptions and generated voice ids, so the chosen one can be saved as a permanent voice. The first five imp candidates vary the premise's voice brief across accent, age and texture. Model: `eleven_ttv_v3`.
- **The ElevenLabs key lives in `.env`** at the project root, which git ignores. The tools read it from there or from `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY`, and never print it. The key in use is scoped and cannot read account usage, so the tools skip that check.
- **The imp's voice is audition 5, the wiseguy barker.** User. A wry, fast, mocking New York swagger, like a bookie egging on a stupid bet. It is saved as a permanent ElevenLabs voice once the account has a free custom voice slot (it was full, 10 of 10).
- **Voiced text inks on in sync with the voice.** User. Each character appears as he says it, from ElevenLabs' per-character timings.
- **Skipping a voiced line.** User, on the dev's recommendation. The first click while he talks shows the whole box at once, but his voice plays on to the end of that box. The next click moves on, fading him out in a fraction of a second if he is still talking. If nobody clicks, the box waits for a click when he finishes.
- **One take per line, played a box at a time.** Dev. Each line is voiced as a single take, so the delivery flows across its sentences. The game plays only the part of the take that belongs to the box on screen, using the timings, so any skip lands at the start of a sentence. Clips are looked up by line id and only played if the line's text still matches the text they were made from.
- **The imp's voice is saved, and 1.02 is voiced.** The wiseguy barker is saved as ElevenLabs voice `DNDKPaMcht4cqNBEVMMQ`, recorded in `tools/voice/voices.json`, and the three 1.02 lines are voiced with `eleven_v3`. Dev: voice clips go into git through Git LFS (`assets/voice/**/*.mp3`), so regenerated takes don't bloat the repository; their timing files and the manifest stay plain text. A page's sound stops just before the next page's first word, so its tail never leaks the next sentence.
- **The imp talks 15% faster.** User: the voice was a bit slow. Dev: the `eleven_v3` model ignores ElevenLabs' speed setting (a test take came out the same length), so the game speeds the voice up itself. The dialogue box plays it at `voice_speed` (1.15), and a pitch shift on the Voice bus keeps his pitch, so he sounds like himself talking faster. The text stays in sync because it follows the take's own clock. If the stretch ever sounds artificial, the fallback is re-voicing with `eleven_multilingual_v2`, which honours a speed setting but is less expressive.
- **Background music.** User: their placeholder track plays as the background music. Dev: the `Music` autoload loops one quiet track (-14 dB) under everything, across scene changes, fading it in, and dips it 6 dB while someone talks. The placeholder file stays out of the public repository because its licence is unknown, so a fresh clone is simply silent until real music arrives.
- **No playback speed-up after all.** User: the pitch correction broke the voice. `voice_speed` is back to 1, which also bypasses the pitch shift. A faster delivery has to come from the recording instead: v3 cannot be told to speed up (expressive stability made it slower, and a rapid-fire direction tag gained only 4%), while `eleven_multilingual_v2` at speed 1.15 reads the same line 14% faster. Comparison takes are in `voice_auditions/speed/` for the user to choose.
- **Back to the imp's first takes.** User changed their mind about the rapid-fire takes. The original `eleven_v3` recordings of 1.02 are restored from history, and the imp's voice setup has no stage direction. The tool still supports a direction for later, and the voice signature only includes one when it is set, so the restored takes count as current.

## 2026-10-22: building Chapter 1

- **The imp says less.** User: the dialogue is shortened so it never dumps exposition on the player, keeping the imp's behaviour. Dev: every imp line of Chapter 1 is trimmed, 3096 words down to 2143 (31% shorter): the jokes, the jabs and his refusal to judge stay, the restating and the over-explaining go. The trimmed copy waits for the user's sign off before it is voiced.
- **The credits** are just Goktug Sugar, then the font and music credits. User.
- **Sleepers lie on the rails.** User. Dev: they lie along the near rail, head propped toward the player, eyes closed, z marks drifting up. When hit they only dissolve, since they are already down.
- **A run player plays the chapters.** Dev. It walks a chapter's beats in order (cinematics, narration, questions) and owns what happens between them. A secret ending is the imp's reaction as his last line, a beat's pause, the stage dissolving and the imp fading together, then the credits. The question loop stops after the reaction on an ending and leaves the stage for the run player. Cinematics are a new beat kind that names a scene.
- **Souls are kinds of one figure, as data.** Dev. Every soul shares one base (fold, then dissolve), and each kind is a small file in `figures/kinds/` that content names: which drawing, which colour, what size, which pose and which details. Variety comes from details on the same stick figure (closed or hidden eyes, a belly, a cane, tufts of hair, a heart, z marks), never new art. The one exception is the baby, a swaddled bundle. The pregnant woman rests a hand under her belly and the elderly have a bald crown with a tuft over each ear: the first tries read as an arm and as horns.
- **The heart and the z marks step with the boil.** Dev. Like breathing, they move on the page clock and redraw with the boil instead of every frame, so they read as hand-drawn animation.
- **The trolley stage reads more from its data.** Dev. Pleas (`says` on a soul: clasped hands, words inked on over the head, unvoiced for now), roped souls (a brown rope at the waist, gone at the first hit), souls turned away (no eyes), a dark track (a heavy ink wash with a scribbled edge that hides the track and the trolley, lifting after the roll) and a rolling trolley (it creeps toward the split while the player decides and slows so it never arrives on its own). The dark stops short of the imp's wing, so a trolley sent into it stops unseen inside it instead of running out the far side.
- **Three tracks make a fan the player points into.** Dev. With no empty rail and no default, a lever means nothing, so the routes fan out up, straight and down, read top to bottom as first, second and third. Pointing at a route lights it amber, clicking picks it, and the choice row lights the same route.
- **Dialogue pages leave room for a space.** Dev, a bug fix. A page was sized to its widest line, but a line only stays whole with room for the space it broke at, so a long page could spill its last word onto a line below the frame. A test now shows every page of the content at its given size.
- **The opening crash.** Dev, from the script's brief, fragmentary flash of the judge's death. Pale ink on black: headlights bear down on a night road, flickering at 9 frames a second with frames lost to black; the view swerves; the hit lands with a white flash and a shake; black; then the eyes open, the dark clearing in uneven blotches that blink, drift and sharpen. No text, no body. The headlights' glow is light rather than ink, so it is drawn without the ink material and falls off smoothly instead of in bands. A press skips the crash, but not in its first 0.6 seconds, so the click that brings the window into focus does not throw the opening away.
- **The credits roll from data.** Dev. `content/credits.json` holds the title and the credits; the page fades in, they scroll up (each role in amber over its name), and a press hurries them. Besides Goktug Sugar, the font and the music, the file credits ElevenLabs for the imp's voice and Godot Engine, as is customary; the music line reads "Placeholder track" until the real music arrives.
- **Would you rather is two doodles.** Dev. A doodles stage puts two small ink doodles side by side over their labels, with "or" between them. Pointing lifts a doodle and clicking picks it, the same as the choice row. On the pick an amber loop circles it, the other doodle dissolves and the pick hops. Each doodle is a script in `doodles/` that content names by id. The doodles' colours wait on the user (see `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`).
- **The ink noise is built on first use.** Dev, a bug fix. A script the editor reloads comes back with its static variables empty, so during a headless import the brush's noise table was empty while the editor redrew the ink, and every import logged about 19,600 out of bounds errors. The brush now builds the table the first time it needs it.
- **The doodles keep their colours.** User: sky blue wings on the flying figure, grey rewind and pause buttons, a gold duck with an orange bill, brown horses and a brown poop, a pink scoop on an orange cone.
- **The credits name Godot but not ElevenLabs.** User: Goktug Sugar, the font, the music and Godot Engine.
- **The trimmed copy is signed off and voiced.** User. Dev: Chapter 1's 58 imp lines, 1.02 included, are voiced in the wiseguy barker's voice with `eleven_v3`, about 11,100 characters. The pleas stay unvoiced for now.
